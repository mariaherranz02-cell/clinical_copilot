DOCUMENT 1
Title: Candidiasis overview

Candidiasis is an infection caused by fungi of the genus Candida. Candida albicans is a common cause of mucosal and invasive candidiasis, but other species such as Candida glabrata, Candida tropicalis, Candida parapsilosis, Candida krusei, and Candida auris can also cause disease.

Invasive candidiasis can occur when Candida enters the bloodstream or internal organs. Risk factors include prolonged hospitalization, intensive care, central venous catheters, recent surgery, broad-spectrum antibiotic exposure, and immunosuppression.

Candida auris is of particular concern because some strains can show resistance to multiple antifungal drugs and because the organism can persist on surfaces and spread in healthcare environments.


DOCUMENT 2
Title: Candida auris diagnosis

Candida auris can be difficult to identify using some conventional biochemical identification systems. Accurate species-level identification may require methods such as matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry or molecular methods.

When Candida auris infection is suspected, appropriate laboratory identification is important because treatment and infection-control decisions may depend on the species and its antifungal susceptibility profile.


DOCUMENT 3
Title: Candida auris treatment

Treatment of invasive Candida auris infections depends on the clinical situation and antifungal susceptibility.

Echinocandins are commonly recommended as initial therapy for many adults with invasive Candida auris infection when susceptibility results are not yet available.

Antifungal susceptibility testing can be important because resistance to antifungal agents has been reported in Candida auris. Treatment should be guided by clinical assessment, laboratory results, and applicable clinical guidelines.


DOCUMENT 4
Title: Candida infection risk factors

Important risk factors for invasive candidiasis include recent abdominal surgery, central venous catheter use, prolonged intensive care, total parenteral nutrition, broad-spectrum antibiotic exposure, renal replacement therapy, and immunosuppression.

The presence of risk factors does not by itself establish a diagnosis of invasive candidiasis. Clinical findings, microbiological testing, and the overall clinical context are required.


DOCUMENT 5
Title: Candida auris infection control

Candida auris can spread in healthcare environments through contaminated surfaces and equipment and through contact between people.

In healthcare settings, infection-control measures can include appropriate hand hygiene, cleaning and disinfection of the environment and reusable equipment, and precautions for patients colonized or infected with Candida auris according to local protocols.

Because Candida auris can persist on surfaces, environmental cleaning is an important component of infection prevention.
